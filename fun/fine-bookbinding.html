Fine Bookbinding

Over the past couple of years I've been teaching myself a more traditional form of bookbinding using leather covers. Once the leather is on the book, I use antique brass stamps, heated up and pressed into the covers and spine — sometimes with gold foil, sometimes just blind. It's been a fun learning curve.

Overview

In 2023 I realized I needed more creative hobbies, ones that weren't just consumption (media, products, etc). I wanted a challenge, so I decided to try to level up my bookbinding skills. The result is that I've steadily built a collection of equipment that is both physically and monetarily hefty. You might say that I'm in too deep to ever give it up. But few things are more enjoyable than losing oneself for hours stamping decorations in book covers.

Tools

In order to decorate the covers, I use a variety of stamps made of brass. They get heated up and pressed into the leather, which creates the marks you see. Many of the stamps (called finishing tools) I own are upwards of 100 years old. Some are even 200! The age can be estimated if the tool has a maker's mark on the shank. It's humbling to know that I'm working with the same equipment used by book binders for generations. I felt they needed proper storage, so I converted an antique sewing box I found on Marketplace to do the job.
